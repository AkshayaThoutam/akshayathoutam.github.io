My clinical experiences, combined with my training in clinical research, shape how I approach AI and machine learning by grounding my technical work in biological and clinical reality. Through shadowing and volunteering, I developed an appreciation for disease heterogeneity and patient-to-patient variability, which motivates building models that capture complex structure in biological data rather than relying on simplified assumptions. I prioritize methods that generalize across datasets, are robust to technical variation, and can be evaluated through biologically and clinically meaningful tasks, aiming to develop AI methods that are computationally strong, statistically principled, and translationally relevant.
